Sometimes money arrives before you've invoiced for it, or a customer pays more than they owe. Spine keeps whatever doesn't go on an invoice as credit for that customer. The credit sits on the payment until you apply it to an invoice or refund it, so nothing is lost and nothing is counted twice.

## Hold all or part of a payment as credit

<Steps>

1. **Start a payment** — Go to **Payments** and press **Record payment**, then choose the **Customer**.

2. **Enter the amount received** — Type the full **Amount**, then tick only the invoices it pays, or none at all. If the customer has no open invoices, the dialog says the whole payment becomes their credit.

3. **Read the total line** — It says, for example, "$1,000.00 goes on 1 invoice; $250.00 becomes credit for Maple Ridge Homes." If nothing is ticked, it says the whole amount becomes credit, to apply to an invoice later or refund.

4. **Record it** — Press **Record payment**. The toast says how much is credit for the customer.

</Steps>

<Screenshot id="payments-record-credit" alt="The Record a payment dialog, which says anything over what it pays becomes the customer's credit" />

The payment's page then reads **Credit left** next to its receipt number, and its strip shows how much is left **To apply or refund**. On the **Payments** list, press the **Credit to use** card to see only payments with credit left.

## Pay a deposit invoice

If you sent a deposit (advance) invoice to request money up front, record the payment against it like any other invoice. It's offered in the list under **What it pays**, marked **Deposit**.

- Paid in full, the deposit invoice reads **Paid**. Part-paid, it reads **Partially paid** with what's still outstanding.
- The money is still held as the customer's credit, because the work isn't invoiced yet. When you send the final invoice, apply the deposit to it. See [Apply customer credit to an invoice](../apply-customer-credit-to-an-invoice/).
- The deposit carries no tax. The final invoice's lines carry the full tax.

<Aside type="tip">
  Credit can also come from a credit note worth more than what was still owed on the invoice. That extra becomes the customer's credit too, and is offered wherever you apply or refund credit.
</Aside>

## What happens in your books

The part of a payment that goes on invoices reduces what customers owe you. The part held as credit goes to Customer Advances until it's applied or refunded.
